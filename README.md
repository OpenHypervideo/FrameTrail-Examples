# FrameTrail-Examples
Demos and Examples of FrameTrail Projects

### Usage 

- In your FrameTrail installation, replace your `_data` directory with one of the `[project]/_data` directories in this repository. 
- Log in with `demo@frametrail.org` : `demodemo` 

### Projects

- [Explore the Picture (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/explore-the-picture/) - A slow walk through Pieter Bruegel the Elder's "Netherlandish Proverbs" (1559). Outlines follow the scenes as the camera drifts past them; click one to find out which proverb it shows. A ring of pictures around the video holds all 18 scenes there are to find.
- [The Weather, in Objects (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/weather-in-objects/) - A weather forecast for an invented island, without a video file: the map is a picture, and the clouds, temperatures, charts and warnings are overlays that the player animates in time, with a narrator and captions in English and German. It starts by asking where you will be tomorrow.
- [The Room Decides (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/the-room-decides/) - A journey from the surface of the sea, up into the sky or down into the deep. At two forks the film stops and asks where to go. Alone, click an answer; in a room, click the phone button in the player controls and let everyone scan the QR code: the phones vote, and the majority decides.
- [Fold-Along (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/fold-along/) - A paper boat in nine steps. The film stops after every step and waits until you have folded it: watch the step again, or go on. Pair a phone with the phone button in the player controls and lay it beside your paper: it shows the steps and a tip for each.
- [One Film, Every Viewer (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/one-film-every-viewer/) - A wordless short film with what different viewers need beside it: captions for its sounds in English and German, written descriptions of what happens, a transcript to search, and a paired phone as one viewer's own screen for them. A second version stops at the more important descriptions, to give time to read them.
- [As Long as You Like (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/as-long-as-you-like/) - One film, three lengths. ESO's film about the zodiacal light asks how much time you have and plays the essentials (1:15), the essentials with their explanations (3:13), or all of it (6:22). The parts a version leaves out are marked in the chapter list, and each can be kept as it comes up.
- [Into the Black Hole (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/into-the-black-hole/) - NASA's simulated flight into a supermassive black hole, with the explanations as a layer on top: notes that pause the film, two small films of the clocks and the camera's path playing in step, captions in English and German, and a switch that takes the notes away.
- [A Warming World, Year by Year (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/a-warming-world/) - NASA's map of global surface temperature, one year per second from 1880 to 2025, with instruments: the year's figure as a counter, the record as a line that grows in step, and stops at the years that stand out. It starts in the decade you were born.
- [Layers of a Painting (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/layers-of-a-painting/) - Hieronymus Bosch's "Adoration of the Magi" as a photograph, an infrared reflectogram and an X-ray, in three films that move together. A lens under the pointer shows one layer inside the other, and a tour stops where the layers differ.
- [Many Voices (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/many-voices/) - The film of Bruegel's "Netherlandish Proverbs" from Explore the Picture, annotated by three authors who each see something different in it: an art historian, a folklorist and a nine-year-old. Each author's notes are a layer of their own, shown as a row under the film and as a tab beside it.
- [A Map as the Story (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/a-map-as-the-story/) - Ten places seen from orbit, most of them over four decades, opened from a map of the world: cities that spread, glaciers that retreat, a delta that comes back. Each pin opens a time-lapse of satellite pictures from NASA with notes and a locator, and the map marks the places you have seen and shows one theme at a time.
- [How We Know: The Beirut Port Explosion (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/how-we-know-beirut/) - Forensic Architecture's reconstruction of the explosion of 4 August 2020, streamed from Vimeo, with the method, the sources and the findings of each step as three layers of annotations beside the film.
- [Tracking the Ghost Fleet (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/tracking-the-ghost-fleet/) - Scripps News and Bellingcat follow ships that carry grain out of occupied Crimea with their transponders off. Beside the film, streamed from YouTube: how satellite images, ship-tracking data, a network analysis and a ship-spotter on the Bosphorus fit together, who speaks, and what was found.
- [Two Trails: The Route of the Buk (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/two-trails-mh17/) - The Joint Investigation Team's animation of how the missile launcher that shot down flight MH17 was brought to its launch site and taken away, streamed from YouTube, with the evidence the investigators name and the earlier open-source findings as layers beside it.
- [What is FrameTrail? (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/what-is-frametrail/) - The front page of frametrail.org as a film without video: what FrameTrail is, its features, use cases and principles in the page's own words, each with a small demonstration, and the materials it points to beside the stage.
- [FrameTrail Explainer (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/frametrail-explainer/) - FrameTrail explained by a film that has no video file: six hypervideos made only of overlays animated on an empty timeline. The opening asks what you want to make, each answer leads to its own film (teaching, reporting, research, storytelling), and all four end where the film hands you the editor.
- [Zoom in on DNA (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/zoom-in-on-dna/)
- [Companion Screen (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/companion-screen/) - Zoom in on DNA with the annotations and the transcript on a paired phone: open it on a computer, click the phone button in the player controls and scan the QR code with your phone.
- [DNA Quiz (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/dna-quiz/) - Zoom in on DNA as a quiz for one viewer or a whole room: six questions, each asked right after the part that answers it. Click the phone button in the player controls and let everyone scan the QR code: the room votes on its phones, the majority decides, and a wrong answer plays that part again. A strip below the video, and on every phone, shows what has been answered.
- [Solar System Quiz (2026)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/solar-system-quiz/) - An explainer video with six questions, each asked right after the part that answers it; a wrong answer plays that part again.
- [Field Trip (2019)](https://openhypervideo.github.io/FrameTrail-Examples/field-trip/) - The interactive documentary about Tempelhofer Feld in Berlin, a ronjafilm production (credits at the [original](https://en.fieldtrip.berlin)), rebuilt from FrameTrail data and one small extension: an intro, 14 episodes as characters on a map of the field, animated figures in each episode that lead into another one, and the wind that carries you there. English version; the videos stream from the original's video host.
